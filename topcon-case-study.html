LTIMINDTREE / MOBILE UX

Topcon Field Mobile

Designing a mobile experience that helps field engineers capture surveying measurements, manage site activities, and work reliably in low-connectivity environments.

MY ROLE

Senior UX/UI Designer

UX TIMELINE

2 months

PROJECT TYPE

B2B · GIS

PLATFORM

Web & Mobile

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Complex fieldwork. A clearer mobile experience.

Enhancing the existing Topcon mobile experience required more than redesigning screens. We first had to understand the Total Station, its measurement workflows, and the needs of engineers working in demanding field conditions.

01

Understanding the device

Learning how the Total Station, prism, and measurement calculations worked before translating technical processes into understandable mobile interactions.

TECHNICAL COMPLEXITY
02

Different user needs

Designing appropriate workflows for field engineers, site managers, project managers, and contract personnel, each with different responsibilities and information needs.

USER PERSONAS
03

Working in the field

Making measurements, stockpile mapping, inspections, and task management practical on mobile, including workflows that could continue offline and synchronise when connectivity returned.

FIELD USABILITY

02 / MY APPROACH

From understanding the device to designing for the field.

I focused on understanding the technology and the people using it before designing the interface. Through client collaboration, low-fidelity prototyping and field validation, we refined the experience around real surveying workflows.

01

Learn how the device works

Worked closely with the client to understand the Total Station, prism, measurement readings and the calculations behind the surveying process. This initial learning phase helped us design around the actual technology rather than assumptions.

DEVICE UNDERSTANDING
02

Understand personas and task flows

Considered the different responsibilities of field engineers, site managers, project managers and contract personnel. Their tasks and information needs informed the structure of the mobile experience.

PERSONAS & USER FLOWS
03

Sketch, prototype and validate

Explored information architecture and interactions through sketches, paper prototypes and low-fidelity wireframes. We reviewed the concepts with the client and obtained approval before progressing to detailed UI design.

LOW-FIDELITY PROTOTYPING
04

Collaborate and test in the field

Collaborated face-to-face with the client and Japanese UX designers, refining the experience through agile iterations. On-site field usability testing helped us evaluate whether engineers could understand and use the proposed mobile workflows in their working environment.

FIELD USABILITY TESTING

03 / KEY WORKFLOWS

Connecting field measurements to project operations.

The mobile experience brought together surveying measurements, map-based site activities, workforce coordination and reporting. Each workflow was designed around the practical needs of teams working in the field.

01

Surveying & measurement capture

Connected the mobile workflow with the Topcon Total Station to receive field readings, support measurement calculations and generate the required outputs.

DEVICE INTEGRATION
02

Map-based site operations

Used the client-requested ArcGIS mapping experience with point, line, circle and polygon tools to select site areas and identify relevant stockpile locations.

GIS & MAPPING
03

Dashboards & task assignments

Structured workflows for field engineers, site managers and project managers, covering assigned tasks, inspection progress, check-in/check-out and workforce coordination.

FIELD OPERATIONS
04

Offline work & reporting

Supported measurement capture in areas without internet access, followed by data synchronisation when connectivity returned and report generation for the project management team.

OFFLINE & SYNC

04 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Designing for the realities of fieldwork.

Every design decision was informed by the technical workflow, the people using the application, and the conditions in which they worked. The focus was on making complex field operations easier to understand and perform on mobile.

01

Make technical data understandable

Structure measurement readings and supporting information so engineers can interpret the results and understand the next step in the surveying workflow.

02

Design around field conditions

Account for on-site tasks, map-based area selection and intermittent connectivity, including offline measurement capture and synchronisation when a connection returns.

03

Adapt to different user roles

Organise information and task flows around the responsibilities of engineers, site managers and project managers, while maintaining consistency with the client's existing design system.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Understand the work before designing the interface. Learning how the Total Station operated and how engineers worked on-site helped us make more informed UX decisions.

05 / OUTCOME & REFLECTION

From technical complexity to a clearer UX direction.

The project brought together device understanding, role-based workflows and field-focused mobile design. The UX deliverables were completed and handed over within the two-month project period, ahead of the broader timeline.

WHAT I LEARNED

Understand the technology before designing the experience.

Learning how the Total Station, prism and measurement process worked taught me to approach technical products with curiosity before moving into interface design. Direct client collaboration, iterative prototyping and on-site field usability testing helped ground design decisions in the realities of fieldwork.
